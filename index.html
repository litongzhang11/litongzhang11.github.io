02  /  Research

Research

Job Market Paper

Reference-Dependent Sentiment

Litong Zhang. Sole authored.

Abstract

I examine whether social media discourse amplifies the disposition effect in equity markets. Using approximately 200 million StockTwits posts from 2008 to 2024, I construct a daily measure of the share of reference-dependent language that is gain-framed or loss-framed, based on an LLM-supervised classification. I find that gain-dominant discourse increases aggregate turnover in up-markets, whereas loss-dominant discourse reduces turnover in down-markets. Around earnings announcements, sorting on the earnings surprise and pre-event GainTalk yields significant Fama-French five-factor alphas for a high-GainTalk disposition spread, whereas the reverse low-GainTalk spread earns alphas indistinguishable from zero. Overall, the evidence indicates that reference-dependent sentiment acts as a state-dependent amplifier of the disposition effect in trading and in prices around earnings news.

Working Papers

Memorable Expectations and Asymmetric Market Reactions to Earnings Surprises

Litong Zhang. Sole authored.

Abstract

This paper studies how the memorability of analyst expectations affects market reactions to earnings news. Using a large sample of analyst consensus forecasts and earnings announcements, I show that round-number forecasts create salient benchmarks that systematically alter how investors process earnings surprises. Firms with round-number consensus forecasts face a higher implicit performance hurdle, so announcement returns are lower when earnings merely meet expectations. At the same time, market reactions become more sensitive to the size of positive earnings surprises when expectations are anchored at round numbers, producing a steeper return–surprise relation on the positive side. The attention attracted by memorable round forecasts also spills over to other firms announcing earnings on the same day: non-round firms experience weaker announcement reactions but subsequently exhibit stronger post-earnings drift, consistent with delayed price adjustment.

Together, these results suggest that salient expectations reshape how earnings information is incorporated into prices, generating asymmetric price reactions at the event while producing delayed price incorporation in the cross-section of non-round firms.

Memorability and the Disposition Effect

Litong Zhang. Sole authored.

Abstract

This paper investigates the effect of memory-induced attention on investor behavior, focusing on the disposition effect: the tendency to sell winning investments while holding onto losing ones. Drawing on associative memory theories (Bordalo, Gennaioli, and Shleifer, 2020), I show that round numbers in stock prices serve as memorable cognitive reference points that amplify the disposition effect. The finding is robust across specifications, highlighting the psychological impact of price roundness on trading behavior and the critical role of reference-point memorability in shaping investor decisions.

Social Gathering and Amplified Familiarity Bias

Litong Zhang. Sole authored.

Abstract

This paper investigates the causal role of contemporaneous social interaction in mutual fund managers’ portfolio choices. Using manually collected information on the Top 30 MBA networks of alumni reunion cohorts, I show that mutual fund managers connected to senior officers (CEO, CFO, and Chairman) at firms increase connected holdings following the MBA alumni reunions. Stock-level performance tests show that fund managers do not achieve significant positive returns on these connected positions after reunion events, favoring the familiarity hypothesis: an increase in contacts leads to a notable surge in buying behavior despite the absence of superior returns. These findings highlight the role of social networks as channels for the formation of behavioral bias.
